  DIAGNOSTIC (post hoc). Why the ratio is the WORSE
  instrument, which is the opposite of what F2 expected.
  The two statements are equivalent -- F1 confirms it at all
  ten rungs -- but they are not equivalent as TRENDS. Near the
  crossing log rho is about -c (log K* - (1/2) log N), so
    log rho ~ -c (log N) (e - 1/2),   e the exponent,
  and the exponent carries a division by log N that the ratio
  does not. That division damps the fluctuation of K* by a
  factor log N without damping the trend by as much:
  mean log N over the ladder            : 13.4291
  exponent scatter times log N          : 0.0524
  the ratio's own scatter               : 0.0689
  -- the same noise, seen through the two lenses.
  exponent trend per doubling times log N: 0.0618
  the ratio's own trend per doubling     : 0.0243
  -- and here they differ, because log rho carries a second
  drift term, -c (e - 1/2) d log N, that the exponent has
  divided away. The lesson of {#rem:muvscoin} does not
  generalise: a ratio at a fixed abscissa beats a fitted
  exponent when the exponent is a SLOPE THROUGH POINTS, and
  loses to it when the exponent is itself a ratio whose
  denominator grows.
