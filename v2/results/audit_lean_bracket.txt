  And the drift of the constant those brackets extrapolate,
  which gate check G33 reads. Within a law the constant is the
  decay exponent b, and the bracket is built from its own
  leave-one-out spread rather than from an assumed wobble, so

  b runs 0.1673 to 0.1880, a relative spread of 0.1180
DRIFT lean_exponent_b 0.1180
  The wider of the two brackets above is not drift at all but
  model ambiguity: the power law and the log law are not
  separable over the accessible range, and that is a choice
  between shapes, not a wobble in a constant. It is reported
  as a bracket because the reader needs one number, but it
  should not be read as an error bar on a fit.

  DIAGNOSTIC (post hoc). Why a twelve per cent uncertainty in
  the exponent costs whole decades. The forecast solves
  |0.5-f| = c N^{-b} = 0.01, so log N = log(c/0.01)/b: the exponent
  sits in the DENOMINATOR of a logarithm, and a relative error
  d in b multiplies log N by 1/(1-d), i.e. scales N by a power
  of itself.
  relative spread in b            : 0.1242
  so log10 N scales by up to      : 1.1419
  which on log10 N = 14.01 is      : 1.99 decades
  measured one-sided spread       : 0.84 decades
  The measured spread is the smaller of the two because the
  leave-one-out refits the intercept alongside the exponent,
  and a steeper decay comes with a smaller constant, which
  pulls the crossing back. The law choice has no such brake.
