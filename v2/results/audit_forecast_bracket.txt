  DIAGNOSTIC (post hoc). Why the bracket is this wide. At the
  crossing K* = N^e the balance reads, with
  S(K) ~ 2 d sqrt(K)(log K - 2),
    N^{(1-e)/2}  ~  gamma sqrt(log N) (e log N - 2),
  so a relative error d in gamma moves N by (1+d)^{2/(1-e)} --
  the square root of K doubles the exponent, which is the
  factor a naive 1/(1-e) misses.
  e         naive (1.10)^{2/(1-e)}   measured, high side   low
  0.50      1.4641                   2.1814                2.4712
  0.56      1.5422                   2.0600                2.2825
  Even that understates it, because the right-hand side is not
  constant: sqrt(log N)(e log N - 2) GROWS with N, so N must
  move further still to absorb the same change in gamma. The
  logs amplify the error rather than damping it, and that is
  the whole gap between the naive factor and the measured one.
